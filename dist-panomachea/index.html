A card game of mythic proportions

All-out mythological war in this competitive card-capture game. Created and Illustrated by Jordan Holt.

Panomachea (pān-ō-mäk-e-ä) is an original, all-out mythological battle game.

Capture the most cards to win. Fight with the gods, heroes and monsters of Ancient Greek myth.

This is the kind of game that you'll remember playing. It gives you every opportunity to pull off those staggering wins you'll lord over your friends for years, and all but guarantees you'll eventually face the crushing defeat that can only come when you're absolutely sure of victory.

I based the game on a simple concept like War, but with all the changing variables it becomes a complex-but-accessible strategy game with a robust dose of chaos.

But let's talk about the cards.

Not as complex as a deck-builder or as simple as most casual games, this is a "middle ground" strategy card game with a rich tableau of 56 hand-illustrated Fighter Cards, representing a god, hero or beast from the world of Greek mythology. Each one with its own unique abilities, strengths and weaknesses.

But that's not enough to ensure that Panomachea is both approachable for new players and that every hand is a different challenge. So the total 84 card deck includes 24 Tyche (Chance or Fortune) Cards and 4 World Cards that keep the upper hand shifting throughout the game.

Every one of the 56 Fighter Cards has an original interpretation of the Immortal, Hero or mythological Beast, in addition to the character's Power Level in the game, name (both the Latinized version we're familiar with and the Ancient Greek spelling), a brief description and a clearly defined suit by the color of the card and the emblem in the bottom right.

There are four suits in Panomachea: the Sky, Sea, Earth and Fire.

Playing Panomachea is an experience brimming with color and character that can only come from an artist who is passionately invested in the beauty and uniqueness of every card.

But for the moment, let's talk about the art.

The 67 unique card illustrations you'll find in this 84 card deck were drawn in their entirety by me. Not one pixel is generated by AI. Neither were the references or concepts used.

I drew each card using photo references of real people and animals I purchased from terrific suppliers like Grafit Studio and from photos of Classical art like the Samothrace's Winged Victory.

As an enthusiast of Classical history and an ancient mythology obsessive, I've tried to give these card designs a strong degree of cultural fidelity —even using Greek pottery and sculpture to guide the character designs— while also giving the characters new color and flavor to better distinguish them from one another and make them visually striking.

My goal was to make a card game that is not only extremely fun and challenging to play, but also make playing a sheer delight because every card is as beautifully designed as I can make it to maximize its quality and appeal.

Panomachea is simple in concept.

Your opponents have cards, and you want them. Because each one of those cards is worth sweet, sweet points.

So you send your Fighter Cards out to capture theirs by having a higher Power Level than the others.

Each player (for example, out of 4) chooses a Fighter Card and plays them in order. There's always a World Card active that boosts one of the four suits. Once all the players have laid down their Fighter Cards, the highest overall Power Level captures all the cards on the field. However, it's not as simple as just having the highest Power Level card. Tyche (Ty-kee) cards, World cards, individual Fighter Card abilities and other captured cards can sway the round in your favor.

World Cards

But since there's always a World Card active that boosts one suit and not the others, and changes through multiple methods throughout the game, there's always advantage and disadvantage on the table.

Tyche Cards

But that's not the only agent of chaos in this deck. Each card also has their individual Power Level, a unique ability that allows advantages against specific cards (for example, Athena steals victory from Zeus, and Odysseus can't be captured by Poseidon), and players also have Tyche (chance or fortune) cards that allow the tide to be turned in their favor by a strategic play.

Tyche Cards (Tyche means Chance or Fortune in Ancient Greek) allow the player to shake up the Field of Battle and employ gambits to lose big or win it all.
